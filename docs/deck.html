The buyer has tried this and been burned. The brief names the reason in one phrase: uneven sensor coverage. Some stations stream full PLC telemetry; a meaningful minority run on a clipboard. A twin that needs every station to report is a twin for a plant nobody has.

Three consequences, and they compound. A slow station starves everything downstream, and you see it stations away and minutes late. A defect introduced early surfaces late, with every unit built in between carrying it. And the person deciding — hold the batch or ship it — is working from incomplete information under time pressure.
02 · What exists

Everything that predicts assumes it can see the line.
Everything that tolerates blindness only reports the past.

Augury Process Health is the closest — AI root cause on multi-causal quality — but it is built for continuous process lines with dense historian coverage, and has no flow model of a discrete line and no answer for a station that reports nothing. The upper-left quadrant is empty, and it is where the brief's plant lives.

03 · How it works

Four layers, and a wall you cannot cheat through.

layerseesdoes
1 · Planteverythingground truth. The real line, or a discrete-event simulation of one
2 · Sensorseverythingforwards only what each station's profile allows — full PLC, cycle timestamps, manual checklist, or nothing at all. Carries real noise: jitter, clock offset, dropouts, latency
3 · Loomonly what layer 2 passedreconstructs the line, forecasts bottlenecks, catches drift at source, contains defects. Tags every value ● measured ◐ inferred ○ simulated
4 · Evaluatorplant and twinscores every prediction against what actually happened. Ships in the product

The wall is architectural, not a promise: sensors.py is the only path from the plant to the twin, so the twin cannot accidentally use data a real line would not have. It is also the integration boundary — the one module a real deployment swaps for an OPC UA or Modbus tap.

A threshold alarm beats us on lead time — by firing 142 times a shift. Every comparator above is fed the same sensor-filtered event stream the twin saw, so the difference is the mechanism and not the data.

06 · Where the AI sits

Statistics produce every number.
The model turns numbers into decisions.

Briefings

One evidence pack, three audiences — shift handover, containment memo, weekly summary. Every figure traceable to a field.

What-if

The model proposes mitigations from a fixed menu; the simulator measures and ranks them. Floater at B3 → 54 veh/h against a 46 baseline.

The improvement loop

Propose → backtest → gate. Two changes that raised lead time were refused for breaking the false-alarm budget. The gate decides, not the proposer.

Onboarding

"18 stations, takt 72 s, 4 manual, 2 dark" → a plant file the loader validates. Extending to another line is a config diff.

Never: predict, score a vehicle, decide a hold, change a live threshold, or write a number that is not in the evidence pack — which is content-hashed before the model sees it.

07 · Trust

It keeps score of itself, in public, including when that hurts.

0.5 – 0.74154%

Confidence means something because it is checked. The evaluator ships in the product, so the plant gets this table for its own line — which is also the gate between shadow mode, advisory, and reversible automation.

What the discipline costs us. Our 5-seed benchmark reported zero false alarms on two scenarios. At 20 seeds they were 2.4 and 1.3 per 8 h — the old numbers were noise, and re-running found a real alert-flooding bug: when a root alert cleared, every station still physically blocked by the constraint raised its own alarm. Fixed; every scenario is now inside budget. We would rather show you this than the earlier number.

Bought from OT operating budget, not capital, which is exactly why a read-only tap clears where a retrofit waits for a window.

The claim is not the number. It is that after two weeks of shadow mode the plant has its own number in place of ours.
09 · Rollout

Read-only, reversible, and gated on evidence at every step.

Weeks 0–4 · Shadow

Read-only tap. Alerts hidden from the floor. The evaluator scores them anyway, and the plant gets its own trust ledger before it is asked to believe anything.

Weeks 4–10 · Advisory

Alerts and holds with their evidence. Confirm/dismiss recalibrates per-station thresholds. First value-of-information retrofits, in windows that already exist.

Weeks 10–16 · Reversible

Flag a batch for inspection; open a hold. Nothing that touches the physical line is ever automatic.

Q2+ · Second line

A new line is a YAML file. A 30-station plant already runs on the same code with zero changes.

Loom never writes to a PLC. Proven against Factory I/O — third-party equipment we did not write — over a real Modbus socket: zero writes. Every recommendation, every override with its note, every AI report and the hash of the evidence it was written from is in an audit table.

10 · What we do not claim

The limits, before you find them.

  • Two adjacent checklist stations cannot be told apart. The twin abstains rather than guess. On our 30-station plant, darkening the one instrumented station in a checklist run takes the forecast to zero in a single step — flow reconstruction needs at least one neighbour with trustworthy timestamps.
  • A defect with no upstream signal is only learnable from inspection failures, so containment for it trails the first catch. On the two-condition scenario the cause is ranked first in 17 of 20 runs, but the hold that follows is poor.
  • The drift onset back-fill recovers no vehicles on our demo scenario. We found this by building the ablation table, and we have removed the claim rather than keep it.
  • The shifting-bottleneck scenario misses 3 of 40 faults, and the healthy-line floor sits slightly above our own published budget rather than under it.
  • Process parameters are proven on the simulated plant only. The flow layer has run against third-party equipment; the quality layer has not.
The ask: one line, one shadow period, four weeks. We bring the tap, the twin and the scorekeeper. The plant brings the sensors it already has.
