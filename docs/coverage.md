# Coverage degradation — what the twin still delivers as the plant goes blind

Generated by `python -m loom.coverage --seeds 3`. Stations are darkened
deterministically: **the faulting station first**, then every k-th of the rest so the
dark stretches are spread rather than clustered. Darkening bystanders is the easy case
and proves nothing.

## Reading these two tables

The first line is the brief's stated mix — a majority of stations well instrumented — and
it degrades rather than breaks: the warning survives at 10, 20 and 30 % dark, with the
failing station itself unlit, at a cost of about a minute of lead.

Note which column *doesn't* move. Reconstruction error holds at ~0.2 s all the way to 50 %
dark, where the forecast has already stopped firing. The two failures are separable and it
matters which is which: the twin still knows what the dark stations are doing, it runs out
of the *measured* samples the trend fit needs to be confident enough to raise an alert. So
the fix is not a better inference model, it is one more timestamp source — which is a
purchase order, and which is what the value-of-information ranking is for.

The second is a cliff, and the cliff is the more useful result. On `plant_b` the fault sits
at `T04`, the only `plc_full` station in a run of seven `checklist` stations. Darkening it
leaves it bounded by finish-only neighbours with 120 s reporting latency — which is exactly
the case the twin is documented to abstain on, because two adjacent finish-only stations
cannot be told apart. So it raises nothing rather than guessing, and the curve goes to zero
in one step.

That is the boundary of the method, stated as a measurement rather than a caveat: **flow
reconstruction needs at least one neighbour with trustworthy timestamps.** It is also
precisely what the value-of-information ranking exists to fix — on this line it should be
recommending a $30 photo-eye on `T03` or `T05` before anything else.
