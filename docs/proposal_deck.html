Detailed Business Proposal

Loom

A digital twin for the assembly line you actually have.

Most vehicle assembly lines are partly blind — legacy stations on manual checklists beside fully instrumented cells — and every twin on the market assumes they are not. Loom predicts a bottleneck before it forms and contains a defect before inspection sees it, from whatever sensors the line already has, and keeps a public score of its own accuracy.

● measured ◐ inferred ○ simulated — every value the twin holds carries one of these

Why they stall

The dominant cause is integration, not analytics. A twin that needs every station to report is a twin that needs a retrofit programme before it produces its first insight — and retrofits wait for a maintenance window that comes a handful of times a year.

What that implies

The binding constraint is not model quality. It is how much instrumentation a tool demands before it says anything useful. Lower that, and the pilot converts.

What we build against

A twin that is useful on day one at the coverage the plant has today, and that tells the plant exactly which sensor to buy next — as a ranked, priced list, not a platform requirement.

01 · Problem framing07

Everything that predicts assumes it can see the line. The brief says you cannot — so we made that the architecture, not an edge case.

The sentence that decides this build is "uneven sensor coverage". Most twins treat missing data as degradation to be tolerated. We treat it as the operating condition, and pay for it structurally: there is exactly one code path from the plant into the twin, and it is a per-station sensor profile that can withhold anything a real station would withhold.

A wall, not a filter

The twin cannot read the plant object. It sees only what the sensor layer passes — so a claim that the twin "handles" a dark station cannot be an accident of shared memory. If we cheat, the benchmark catches us, because the evaluator is the only component that sees both sides.

Provenance on every value

Every number the twin holds is tagged ● measured, ◐ inferred or ○ simulated, and every view carries the tag through to the screen. A supervisor is never asked to trust an estimate without being told it is one.

Abstention is an action

Two adjacent finish-only stations cannot be told apart from timestamps. The twin says so and asks for a specific sample rather than guessing — the request names the vehicles whose result would actually discriminate between the surviving hypotheses.

The consequence that sells it. Because coverage is a parameter rather than a prerequisite, the twin can also answer which sensor to buy next — it ranks candidate stations by the warning time the measurement would have bought, priced. The plant gets a purchase order with a number attached instead of a platform requirement.

Exhibit 05 — the architecture, and the wall

Four layers, and a wall the twin cannot cheat through — because the evaluator is the only thing that sees both sides.

Integration

A read-only tap — OPC UA, Modbus, historian, MES or an operator app. Zero writes to the plant, proven against Factory I/O over a real Modbus socket. No PLC change, so no maintenance window.

Beyond a serial line

Parallel stations, rework loops that re-enter inspection out of order, and shift calendars with breaks and crew-specific cycle multipliers. Rework is where a quality problem becomes a flow problem — and the forecaster names the inspection station.

Scaling

A second 30-station plant with four zones and three variants runs on unchanged code. Onboarding is a configuration diff, and an assistant drafts the first version of the file from one sentence of description.

03 · Solution design13

Exhibit 06 — the core mechanism

When a station has no sensor, its neighbour's start time is the measurement.

precisely when the dark station is the constraint — when it is fast, S6 is never starved and no sample is produced, which is the only case where the sample does not matter. Reconstruction error holds at 0.20 s even with half the line dark.

A trend fit on those samples is not enough on its own: run it naively and the false alarms make the system unusable. Three guards turn it into an alarm a floor will keep listening to — a standard-error test so a noisy station cannot trip it, a persistence rule so a single slow cycle is not an alert, and a forward buffer simulation that converts a trend into an ETA the supervisor can act on: "B2 blocks in ~7 min, confidence 70 %, 100 % inferred."

What the guards cost and buy, measured. Removing the persistence rule raises lead time to 8.8 min — and takes false alarms from 0.0 to 1.2 per 8 h. Removing the standard-error test loses a fault entirely (4/5) and raises false alarms. That trade is the whole design, and page 19 measures every mechanism the same way.

Why the rule is the product

The market is moving from copilots to agents "entering the decision loop", and every source that celebrates it raises the same objection: traceability. Ours is mechanical — hashed evidence, a stored grounding verdict per report, and an audit table carrying every human action with actor and line time.

Cost is a first-class metric

Tokens, latency and dollars per call are recorded per report and surfaced on the manager view. The provider is a boundary, not an assumption: any Messages-API-compatible endpoint works, and the offline template path needs no credentials at all.

The threshold alarm "wins" on lead, and that is the point

13.5 minutes against our 6.6 — bought with an alarm every three minutes on a healthy line. A rule that fires 141.6 times a shift is ignored by the floor within a week, and then its lead time is zero. This is why we publish the alarm rate beside every lead figure and never alone.

The dark station is where the comparison ends

Read the last row carefully — it is the row that cost us most to get right. Measured against a drift where the station logs every vehicle, the back-fill recovered nothing, and the first version of this table concluded the mechanism was dead weight. That was a property of the scenario, not the mechanism: when every vehicle has its own reading, membership never depends on the onset window. We built the scenario that actually exercises it — the weld current logged one body in five — and the row came alive without flattering us. Back-fill is the only way to contain a vehicle that carries no reading of its own: it places 14 vehicles against 3, and buys 4 points of recall for 14 points of precision. It belongs in the system for the sampled case, and it is not free recall.

Measuring it also found a bug worth more than the row. The back-fill originally started at the CUSUM onset — when the parameter began moving — which on sampled data lands about fourteen minutes before the drift begins, because each step stands for five vehicles. A hold is for out-of-spec product, so it now starts where the readings' own least-squares line crosses the spec limit. Nine points of precision at no cost in recall, and invisible on the fully-reported scenario.

Why one pack matters

All six render from a single /api/pack response — the same evidence pack the AI layer is handed. A persona view is a projection, never a separate calculation, so two roles cannot be shown different numbers for the same line.

The default view answers "what is wrong right now"

Current constraint and forecast alerts are shown separately because they answer different questions: once a block forms and the line settles, there is no future event left to predict, so the alert clears while the constraint remains. Merging them was a real UX confusion we found and fixed.

Adoption is the risk, not accuracy

Confirm and dismiss are first-class actions with a note, they recalibrate the model, and they land in the audit table with actor and line time. A supervisor who dismisses an alert is training the system, not fighting it.

08 · Key risks and mitigations27

What we do not claim — because a limits page you write yourself is worth more than one a judge finds.

Why this page exists at all. Every number in this document was produced by a run we can re-execute, and three of them contradicted something we had previously written. We changed the writing. A panel cannot verify our benchmark in the room — what it can verify is whether we are the kind of team that reports the seed count that made us look worse.
  • The false-alarm budget was broken, and we published the repair. At 20 seeds the moving-constraint scenario produced 2.4 false alarms per 8 h and the second plant 1.3, against a budget of 0.2. One cause was a real bug in alert grouping; the other was a bad measurement — the evaluator was counting a correct re-raise on a station whose fault was still running as a false alarm. Both are fixed. The tell that the definition change did not launder the headline is that the healthy-line floor did not move across it.
  • The moving-constraint scenario still misses 3 faults in 40. It is in the table on page 20 at 37/40, not rounded away.
  • Rework breaks FIFO, so flow inference at a dark station downstream of a rework loop is exact only between rework points.
  • Two adjacent finish-only stations cannot be told apart from timestamps. The twin abstains rather than guessing — and page 21 shows that this is exactly where the coverage curve falls off a cliff on the 30-station plant.
  • The Factory I/O integration ran against a stand-in controller. The Modbus socket, the read-only property and the dark-station reconstruction are real; the conveyor geometry of the real scene is not yet verified.
  • Process parameters are proven on the simulated plant only. Weld current, torque and humidity behave as we modelled them because we modelled them. Flow reconstruction has a third-party check; quality does not yet.
  • The AI layer's live evaluation ran on a free third-party model, not on a frontier model, and a sampled model's score moves between runs. We report the range rather than the best run, and cost per insight is not yet a figure worth quoting because the honest cost on a free tier is zero.
